Phase 1 — Upload the .docx to Google Drive
1
Download the document Claude built for you
In this chat, click the download icon next to Supreme_Notary_Feature_Documentation.docx. Save it somewhere easy to find — your Desktop or Downloads folder.
2
Open Google Drive
Go to drive.google.com in your browser. Sign in with your Google account if you aren't already signed in.
3
Upload the file
Click + New (top-left) → File upload → find and select Supreme_Notary_Feature_Documentation.docx from wherever you saved it. Wait for the upload bar to finish.
4
Open it as a Google Doc
Find the file in Drive (it appears with a Word icon). Double-click it → it opens in a preview. Click Open with Google Docs at the top. Google will convert it automatically — all formatting, tables, headings, and code blocks will be preserved.
5
Save a clean copy as a true Google Doc
Once open, go to File → Save as Google Docs. This saves a native .gdoc version in Drive (not the .docx). From this point on, work in the Google Doc version.
Phase 2 — Add your LinkedIn link inside the document
6
Get your LinkedIn profile URL
Go to linkedin.com → click your profile photo (top-right) → View Profile → look at the browser address bar. Copy the full URL — it looks like https://www.linkedin.com/in/yourname. That is your link.
7
Decide where the link goes in the document
The two best places are: (A) The cover page — below "Supreme Mobile Notary Service" where your name would go, or (B) The closing page — at the bottom, under the final quote. Either works perfectly for a capstone.
8
Type your link text in the document
Click where you want the link. Type something professional like: Connect on LinkedIn or View My LinkedIn Profile. Highlight that text by clicking and dragging over it.
9
Insert the hyperlink
With the text highlighted, press Ctrl + K (Windows) or ⌘ + K (Mac). A small box appears — paste your LinkedIn URL into it. Press Enter or click Apply. The text turns blue and underlined — it is now a live, clickable link.
10
Test the link
Hold Ctrl (Windows) or ⌘ (Mac) and click the blue link text. Your LinkedIn profile should open in a new browser tab. If it does, it is working perfectly.
Phase 3 — Share the Google Doc with a working link
11
Open the Share settings
In your Google Doc, click the big Share button in the top-right corner. A sharing dialog box will open.
12
Change access to "Anyone with the link"
In the dialog, look for General access at the bottom. Click the dropdown that says Restricted → change it to Anyone with the link. Then set the permission to Viewer (so people can read but not edit). Click Done.
13
Copy the shareable link
Click Share again → at the top you will see Copy link. Click it. You now have a shareable URL on your clipboard that anyone can open. This is the link you paste into LinkedIn, emails, or your capstone submission.
Phase 4 — Post the document link on LinkedIn
14
Go to LinkedIn and start a post
Open linkedin.com → click Start a post at the top of your feed. This opens the post composer.
15
Write your caption
Write a short, proud caption. For example: "I just completed my capstone project — I built 3 features for my mobile notary business including a live booking system powered by Supabase and an instant price calculator. Here is the full documentation 👇" Then paste your Google Doc link on the next line.
16
Post it
Click Post. LinkedIn will automatically generate a preview card from your Google Doc link. Your network will be able to click it, open the document, and see everything you built — including the working LinkedIn link inside the document itself.
Pro tip: After posting on LinkedIn, copy the LinkedIn post URL and paste it back into your Google Doc on the cover page as a second link labeled "View my LinkedIn post about this project." Now the document and the post point to each other — a professional loop that shows capstone reviewers and future clients exactly who you are.
Heads up: If someone opens your Google Doc link and sees "Request access" instead of the document, you need to go back to Step 12 and make sure "Anyone with the link" is selected — not "Restricted."
